WHERE THINGS STAND

Four things worth knowing before you read further.

01 · THE PROBLEM

Nobody is in the room at 3am.

Bedridden patients who cannot call for help rely on scheduled rounds. Between rounds, pain goes unreported. Forty-one seconds, not four hours, is the difference we care about.

02 · THE APPROACH

One camera, three frozen models.

A face is detected, described as 256 numbers, and read over a rolling second of video. Above a threshold, a carer's phone rings. The device does not learn the patient.

03 · WHERE IT IS NOW

A laptop, a webcam, and a CAD file.

Face detection and pose detection both work, and each runs on its own today; there is a pose demo on the media page. The current work is bringing both into one live pipeline that feeds the carer dashboard. The CNN and LSTM that would turn a face into a pain score are planned for weeks five and six, and the 8cm cube is at CAD stage: designed, not yet fabricated. Week 9 is a software demo. We're not pretending otherwise.

04 · WHAT'S NEXT

Live video in, alerts out.

Four weeks to the showcase: threshold tuning against recorded sessions, alert latency, and an honest account of where the dataset stops resembling a bedroom.
