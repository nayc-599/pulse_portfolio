Last week we showed Pulse live at the Monash DeepNeuron showcase: a bedside monitor that watches for pain and unusual movement and alerts a nurse, running end to end on one laptop.

Visitors loved pulling their best pain faces to set off an alert. It quickly turned into a competition for the highest pain score (PSPI).

One visitor suggested we look into remote photoplethysmography (rPPG): using the camera to pick up tiny colour changes in the skin as blood flows, to estimate heart rate without touching the patient. It could take Pulse from reading expressions to reading vital signs.
