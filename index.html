WHERE THINGS STAND

Four things worth knowing before you read further.

01 · THE PROBLEM

Nobody is in the room at 3am.

Four hours. That is how long a bedridden patient who cannot call for help may wait between scheduled rounds. We are building toward an alert that closes that gap to something closer to forty-one seconds.

02 · THE APPROACH

Two branches: a face, or a body.

In bed, a blanket hides the body, so the only signal left is the face: 256 numbers read over a rolling second of video. Out of bed, the body is visible instead, so the plan is to read its position. Above a threshold, a carer's phone rings. The face branch runs today. The pose branch is designed, not built.

03 · WHERE IT IS NOW

A laptop, a webcam, and a CAD file.

Face detection is the only model actually running. The CNN and LSTM that would turn it into a pain score are still ahead of us, and the 8cm cube is geometry, not plastic yet. We would rather show the project at this stage than wait for one that looks finished.

04 · WHAT'S NEXT

Live video in, alerts out.

Four weeks out: train the CNN, train the LSTM on top of it, wire both into one live loop, and find out what the domain gap between a clinic dataset and a dark bedroom actually costs us. Any one of those steps could slip past the showcase.
