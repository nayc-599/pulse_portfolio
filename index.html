WHERE THINGS STAND

Four things worth knowing before you read further.

01 · THE PROBLEM

Nobody is in the room at 3am.

Four hours. That is how long a bedridden patient who cannot call for help may wait between scheduled rounds. We are building toward an alert that closes that gap to something closer to forty-one seconds.

02 · THE APPROACH

Two branches: a face, or a body.

In bed, a blanket hides the body, so the only signal left is the face: 256 numbers read over a rolling second of video. Out of bed, the body is visible, so a pose model reads it as 17 keypoints instead. Both detectors already run, each on its own. Beyond them: the CNN that turns a face into those 256 numbers is complete, the LSTM that scores pain from them is in development, and turning a set of keypoints into a fall is the next step on the pose branch.

03 · WHERE IT IS NOW

A laptop, a webcam, and a CAD file.

Face detection and pose detection both work, and each runs on its own today; there is a pose demo on the media page. The current work is bringing both into one live pipeline that feeds the carer dashboard. The CNN is complete and generates the 256-number feature vectors; the LSTM that turns those into a pain score is in development this week, and the 8cm cube is at CAD stage: designed, not yet fabricated. We would rather show the project at this stage than wait for one that looks finished.

04 · WHAT'S NEXT

Live video in, alerts out.

Two weeks out: finish the LSTM on top of the CNN, wire everything into one live loop, and find out what the domain gap between a clinic dataset and a dark bedroom actually costs us. Any one of those steps could slip past the showcase.
